The Calendar She Inherits: On a New Director at Zeitz MOCAA and a Programme Set Before Her

Elvira Dyangani Ose begins as Artistic Director of the Zeitz Museum of Contemporary Art Africa today. The exhibitions on the museum’s walls, and its published season to March 2028, were announced before she was named. Two surveys close on Sunday; Gabrielle Goliath’s Elegy opens in London on Thursday; the Stellenbosch Woordfees opens on Saturday. This week asks what a new director inherits, and where a tenure first becomes visible.

Source Ledger Signal Dispatches Long Essay Closing
“Art has the ability to shift perceptions, open minds, and build futures.”
— Koyo Kouoh (1967–2025), quoted on the Zeitz MOCAA website4

Thursday in South Africa, and the Zeitz Museum of Contemporary Art Africa has a new Artistic Director from this morning. Elvira Dyangani Ose’s term began on 1 October, seven weeks after the museum announced the appointment on 12 August and sixteen months and three weeks after Koyo Kouoh died on 10 May 2025.1 The museum has changed the title: the post Kouoh held as Executive Director and Chief Curator is now two roles, with Fawaz Mustapha as Managing Director responsible for operations, finances and governance.2

The museum’s website lists what is on its walls, and the dates are the useful part. Two survey exhibitions, Cauleen Smith’s and Zohra Opoku’s, close on Sunday 4 October, the director’s fourth day.5 Both were announced in 2025, before the appointment existed. The 2026–27 season was announced on 28 May 2026, two and a half months before she was named. Its first item, the residency IMPRINT, opens today, and its listings run to 28 March 2028, with a Level 4 exhibition to 15 August 2027.6 This edition reads the museum’s calendar as a record of who decided what, and when.

Three other occasions fall inside the week. Elegy, which was meant to be South Africa’s pavilion at the Venice Biennale, opens at Ibraaz in London on Thursday 8 October.8 The Toyota Stellenbosch Woordfees opens on Saturday and runs to 11 October.10 And the Biennale itself is 145 days into a run that closes on 22 November, 52 days from now.

Two practices continue from last week. Every dated source here is dated on or before today, and the ledger below says where sources disagree or could not be confirmed. One practice is new: a thread tracker in the ledger records, for each long-running story, its status and the date of the last source that confirms it.

Curated Dispatches

Cauleen Smith — Afflict the Comfortable, Comfort the Afflicted, Closing Sunday

Zeitz MOCAA, Cape Town Closes 4 October Opened 20 November 2025

The survey of the Los Angeles-based artist and filmmaker Cauleen Smith closes on Sunday. Curated by Thato Mogotsi, the museum’s Curator of Contemporary Art, it was Smith’s first major presentation on the African continent, and is on Level 3, Silo Side.7 It takes its title from one of her text banners, a phrase the museum traces to the writer Finley Peter Dunne, who used it satirically about the press. The films shown include Remote Viewing (2011), Pilgrim (2017) and Sojourner (2018).7

Two facts make it worth the last weekend. Artforum’s review records that Smith names Koyo Kouoh among the people and collectives whose “radical forms of generosity” she draws on, and that she is a participant in Kouoh’s Venice exhibition.11 And it is a survey made inside the museum by its own curator, which is the kind of exhibition the museum describes as an ongoing series of in-depth, research-based solo shows.7 Zohra Opoku’s survey, covered last week, closes the same day.

Zeitz MOCAA, Silo District, V&A Waterfront, Cape Town. Open Monday–Sunday, 10:00–18:00, last entry 17:30. Closes Sunday 4 October 2026. zeitzmocaa.museum.

Gabrielle Goliath — Elegy Opens at Ibraaz, London

London Opens 8 October To 17 January 2027

Ibraaz opens Goliath’s Elegy (2015–ongoing) on Thursday 8 October at 93 Mortimer Street, London, running to 17 January 2027. Ibraaz describes it as arriving after its Venice presentation, where the work was shown independently at the Chiesa di Sant’Antonin after the cancellation of the South African pavilion.8 The Venice showing, from 5 May to 31 July, was supported by the Bertha Foundation and made with Ibraaz, ICA Milano and Galleria Raffaella Cortese. It comprised eight video monoliths and three new suites of performances, commemorating Ipeleng Christine Moholane, two murdered Nama women ancestors and the Palestinian poet Heba Abunada, and launched the Elegy Reader with Ibraaz Publishing.9 Milan follows in January 2027.9

The editorial point is about route. This publication’s 5 March edition recorded the work’s removal from a national platform. Its third presentation now sits in a London institution that co-produced the second. Goliath and Ingrid Masondo’s appeal against the North Gauteng High Court’s ruling has no confirmed outcome in the sources read; see the ledger. The work has been shown in three cities without a South African state platform.

Ibraaz, 93 Mortimer Street, London W1W 7SS. Elegy, 8 October 2026 – 17 January 2027. ibraaz.org.

Toyota Stellenbosch Woordfees — “It’s About Time!”

Stellenbosch, Western Cape Opens Saturday 3–11 October

The Woordfees opens on Saturday with a programme the festival counts at 444 items and calls the largest arts festival programme in the country.10 This publication does not cover corporate programming as such, and the festival carries its sponsor in its name. It is here for its origin and its theme. The festival’s own history places its first edition in March 2000 at Stellenbosch University as Nag van Passie, an overnight celebration of Afrikaans and Dutch poetry.12 Its director, Saartjie Botha, gives the 2026 theme a plain argument: time is scarce, and it is high time to invest in the arts.10

The question this publication put to the KKNK in March, what a language festival owes the full community that speaks the language, applies here too. This edition has not read the 444-item programme and does not claim to answer it. The sources read for it confirm only the dates, the theme and the festival’s own account of its origin.

Toyota Stellenbosch Woordfees, Stellenbosch. 3–11 October 2026. Programme and tickets at woordfees.co.za.

Long Essay

The Calendar She Inherits: On a New Director at Zeitz MOCAA and a Programme Set Before Her

On 19 June 2025, forty days after Koyo Kouoh died, the Zeitz Museum of Contemporary Art Africa published its exhibition season for 2025–26. It named four shows: a tribute to the life of the judge and writer Albie Sachs, opening on 24 July; the first museum survey of Zohra Opoku, opening on 11 September; A Protea Is Not a Flower, opening on 16 October; and a survey of Cauleen Smith.13 All four opened. Visitors to the museum over the sixteen months of its leadership vacancy saw a full programme, and every item on it was scheduled by people other than the director who begins today.

The record that shows this is the museum’s own. The 2025–26 season was announced in June 2025. The 2026–27 season was announced on 28 May 2026: IMPRINT, an artist-led studio residency hosting Cape Town publishing collectives in the museum’s Koyo Kouoh Atelier, on view from 1 October 2026 to 28 March 2028; and Turning Towards the Sun, newly commissioned films by Zeresenay Berhane Mehari, Mati Diop, Jim Chuchu, Wanuri Kahiu and The Otolith Group, developed with the African Film and Media Arts Collective led by Julie Mehretu and Mehret Mandefro.6 The film exhibition is curated by Khanyi Mawhayi, supported by Dr Phokeng Setai and Dr Greer Valley, and runs on Level 4 from 10 December 2026 to 15 August 2027; IMPRINT is co-curated by Nathalie Viruly and Valley.6 Dyangani Ose was announced on 12 August.1 The sources do not say whether she was consulted on the 2026–27 season. They do show that it was announced seventy-six days before she was named.

Who made the programme is also on the record. The Smith survey was curated by Thato Mogotsi, the museum’s Curator of Contemporary Art.7 A Protea Is Not a Flower was curated by Mawhayi.14 The Opoku survey was curated by Beata America and Setai.15 Mustapha, who led the museum on an acting basis, is now Managing Director.2 The institution that kept a programme running through the vacancy was a team of named curators and one operations lead.

The Smith survey is the clearest case. It was listed in the June 2025 announcement, forty days after Kouoh’s death, was curated by Mogotsi, and opened on 20 November 2025.7 Artforum’s review records that Smith cites Kouoh as an influence.11 So a programme scheduled in the vacancy carried the predecessor’s name into its own walls. That is one answer to the question of what continuity means when the person who embodied it is gone: it is whatever the remaining staff choose to keep naming.

A museum without a director still has a calendar. The calendar was made by someone.

That is the structural point, and it applies beyond one museum. When a cultural institution loses its leader, public attention goes to the vacancy: how long it lasts, who might fill it, what the board says. The institution’s actual continuity runs elsewhere. It runs through staff who keep commissioning, scheduling and installing. Earlier editions of this publication tracked the length of the vacancy, and in one case miscounted it. They did not track what the museum produced in that time. The museum’s listings show a great deal: five exhibitions in the 2025–26 programme, a fellowship programme, an art club for teenagers, and a following season announced months ahead.4

The appointment itself is stated plainly in the museum’s release. Dyangani Ose will join as Artistic Director; she is currently Artistic Director of the second Public Art Abu Dhabi Biennial (2026–2027).1 The Art Newspaper reports that the job was split between artistic direction and operations, finances and governance.2 The museum approaches its tenth anniversary in 2027.3 None of these facts says what she will programme.

What the calendar does say is where the unknown begins. The Smith and Opoku surveys close on Sunday. A Protea Is Not a Flower closes on 15 November, and The Procession on 25 October.5 Turning Towards the Sun opens on 10 December and runs to 15 August 2027.6 IMPRINT, which opens today in the director’s first hour in post, runs to 28 March 2028. The museum’s published commitments therefore carry it well into 2028. A director’s first visible decisions, as distinct from her first statements, will be the ones that appear after those dates, or that alter them. The sources this edition read list nothing on Level 4 after 15 August 2027, and nothing in the museum’s main galleries after the surveys close.4

This is a limit of the method, not a verdict on the museum. A published calendar records what an institution has chosen to announce. It does not record what is in preparation, or what a new director is changing behind it. But the limit is itself informative. The question that was asked of the vacancy, when will a director be named, has an answer. The question that follows it, what will the director change, has no entry yet in the sources, and an institution’s public record will be its only evidence for some time.

Koyo Kouoh’s name is on the record in two places that are not statements: the Atelier that will host IMPRINT, and a line on the museum’s homepage that gives her dates and describes her as Executive Director and Chief Curator.4 The first is a room in use. The second names a post that no longer exists in that form. What an institution does with its founders’ and predecessors’ names is also part of what it inherits, and also appears first on the calendar and the walls.

The unanswered question is a dated one. After 15 August 2027, when the Level 4 film exhibition ends, what will be on that floor, and who will have decided?

Closing Reflection

The museum’s homepage carries a line in Koyo Kouoh’s memory, with her dates and the title of the post she held: Executive Director and Chief Curator. The post is now two posts. One is held from this morning by Elvira Dyangani Ose. The other is held by Fawaz Mustapha, who ran the museum in the vacancy.

Neither holds the post the line names. The line is still there, as are the Atelier that bears her name and a survey that cites her. The museum has not said, in anything read for this edition, whether the line will change.
